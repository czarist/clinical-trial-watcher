Women Are Asking Questions Our Clinical Trials Have Not Answered

11/23/2026 | Bruce Dorr | Clinical Leader

https://www.clinicalleader.com/doc/women-are-asking-questions-our-clinical-trials-have-not-answered-0001 

The next opportunity in women's health research is not another hormone product. It’s broader and greatly needed: clinical trials that reflect the women we treat, the symptoms they experience, and the outcomes they value.

In my clinical practice, women frequently describe fatigue, brain fog, disrupted sleep, mood changes, and a loss of their former sense of well-being long before they mention sexual desire.

Sexual desire matters, but women are not simply asking for a better libido. They are asking why they no longer feel like themselves. In a recent retrospective study of 332 women receiving individualized testosterone therapy, participants most often named energy and mood as their greatest benefits, ranking both above sexual desire. They also reported improvements in depressive symptoms, irritability, and anhedonia. These results come from a single post-treatment survey with no control group, so they need confirmation in controlled trials.

Yet decades of testosterone research in women have focused primarily on sexual function. Although randomized controlled trials have demonstrated efficacy for hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD) in appropriately selected postmenopausal women, evidence supporting benefits for other commonly reported symptoms remains insufficient.

Despite growing clinical interest and off-label prescribing, the United States still has no FDA-approved testosterone formulation specifically indicated for women. An absence of evidence is not necessarily evidence of absence. Still, clinical enthusiasm and observational findings cannot substitute for rigorous research.

AI speeds drug discovery, but patient access to trials still lags

 11/20/2026  |  Lisa Henderson  |  BioXconomy

https://www.bioxconomy.com/clinical-and-research/ai-speeds-drug-discovery-but-patient-access-still-lags 

Executive says trial recruitment needs an upstream fix to keep pace with AI-driven discovery.

The clinical trials recruitment ecosystem is facing an infrastructure — if not existential — crisis that has nothing to do with finding new molecules and everything to do with finding the right patients. That is the core argument made by Dennis Akkaya, chief growth officer of myTomorrows, who insists that the system built for drug development is not designed to sustain the AI-fueled drug discovery world now emerging.

While AI has captured the industry’s drug discovery imagination for quite some time, investments have grown in neolabs, tech companies, pharma, small research labs, and more. A recent article from McKinsey & Company referenced investor funding in AI-enabled drug discovery reaching $8.40 billion in 2025, up from $4.10 billion in 2023, a compound annual growth rate of 43% since 2023. Most of that investment has yet to reach the clinic, but Akkaya argues the industry should prepare now for when it does. Every new candidate that emerges, however quickly it was discovered, will still need patients and clinical evidence to clear regulatory pathways. “Discovery may be getting faster,” he said. “Access to patients isn’t.”

“AI will super compress discovery to treatment, the question then is, how do patients get into a trial?” Akkaya told BioXconomy. Since the rise of personalized medicine years ago, trials have become more geographically dispersed, for ever-smaller patient populations, with more complex protocol criteria. 11/17/2026 | Unitaid
 
https://unitaid.org/news-blog/access-planning-for-ebola-medicines-moves-ahead-during-clinical-trials/
 
Gilead’s newly announced voluntary licenses could speed access to medicines currently under evaluation for treatment and prevention of Ebola BDV, as Unitaid and partners advance access roadmaps to help affected countries secure supplies if trials are successful.
 
Unitaid welcomes Gilead’s announcement of royalty-free voluntary licensing agreements with Dr. Reddy’s Laboratories, Eva Pharma and Ferozsons Laboratories. The agreements lay the groundwork for wider access to obeldesivir, an investigational antiviral being studied to prevent Ebola Bundibugyo virus disease (BVD). If clinical evidence supports its use and regulators approve it, the three manufacturers across different regions will be able to produce and supply obeldesivir in the 127 countries covered by the licenses.
 
Gilead is also expanding its existing licenses for remdesivir – an antiviral used to treat COVID-19 – to cover Ebola BVD, paving the way for wider access pending clinical trial results.
 
While clinical trials are still underway, Unitaid, WHO, countries, regional and international partners in the Ebola response have developed access roadmaps for candidate Ebola BVD medicines, including obeldesivir and remdesivir. These roadmaps identify the steps stakeholders can take now to help countries secure supplies quickly when and if the medicines are proven to work. As part of this effort, Unitaid is leading the engagement with manufacturers of these two antivirals to secure access commitments to supply all countries in need. PAHO launches REGIA, a platform to explore and compare clinical trial regulatory requirements across the Americas

11/16/2026 | PAHO
 
https://www.paho.org/en/news/16-11-2026-paho-launches-regia-platform-explore-and-compare-clinical-trial-regulatory
 
The Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) has launched REGIA (Regulation for Research in the Americas), a unique open-access digital platform that brings together, organizes, and compares regulatory requirements for conducting clinical trials across 22 countries in the Americas. The platform was developed by PAHO’s Regional Program on Bioethics as part of the Clinical Trial Accelerator initiative.
 
REGIA addresses a common challenge faced by researchers, sponsors and regulatory authorities: information on clinical trial requirements is often dispersed across multiple sources, while regulations vary from country to country, making it difficult to identify and access up-to-date regulatory information.
 
“REGIA brings together key regulatory information on clinical trials in the Americas in one place, making it easier to access national regulations and official sources while promoting greater transparency and understanding of country-specific regulatory requirements,” said Dr. Carla Saenz, PAHO Regional Advisor on Bioethics.
 
Developed in Spanish and English,the platform presents information in a question-and-answer format based on each country's current regulations. Users can explore the regulatory requirements of a specific country, compare information across up to three countries simultaneously, or filter countries according to specific regulatory requirements.
 
Search and comparison results can be downloaded as PDF documents for future reference and use. Access to REGIA is free of charge and no registration is required.

Porto Alegre to Host Oncology Clinical Research Hub

11/16/2026 | Medicina S/A
 
https://medicinasa.com.br/hub-oncologia/
 
The Government of Rio Grande do Sul, the Porto Alegre City Hall, Procempa, LACOG (Latin American Cooperative Oncology Group), and PUCRS have signed a cooperation protocol to establish an Oncology Clinical Research Hub. The initiative aims to increase the number of cancer-related studies conducted in the state, enhance the visibility of available research protocols, and connect eligible patients with access to investigational treatments.
 
“This initiative will help make the state more attractive to clinical trial sponsors and bring the subject of cancer research closer to the general public,” says oncologist Carlos Barrios, director of the Oncology Research Center (CPO) at the Hospital São Lucas da PUCRS.
 
The signing, attended by Porto Alegre Mayor Sebastião Melo, coincides with the 30th anniversary of the CPO, which was celebrated that same evening at an event held at the Instituto Ling. The Hub will begin in Porto Alegre, with plans for expansion throughout the state of Rio Grande do Sul. According to Barrios, the goal is for all active oncology research centers in both the capital and the state to participate.
 
Areas of Focus
 
The Oncology Clinical Research Hub will launch with a pilot project in Porto Alegre, structured around three key areas: public awareness, patient access to clinical trials, and the expansion of research opportunities in the region. The first area involves a public education campaign designed to increase understanding of oncology clinical research and demonstrate that participating in such studies can offer a potential treatment alternative for cancer patients—provided they meet the specific eligibility criteria for each study.
